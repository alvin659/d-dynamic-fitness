What one-on-one training includes

Your first session is an assessment, not a beat-down. We look at how you move, what your training history is, any old injuries and what you want to achieve in the next 12 weeks. From there you get a programme that fits the time you really have, whether that is two sessions a week or five.

  • Movement and fitness assessment covering posture, mobility, strength and conditioning.
  • A written programme that progresses week by week, so you never plateau by guessing.
  • Coached sessions with hands-on technique correction for every lift and drill.
  • Simple nutrition guidance built around Kenyan food you already eat, not imported meal plans.
  • Progress check-ins with measurements, photos and strength numbers.

Why train with a coach instead of alone

Training alone works for some people. For most, it stalls. A coach removes the guesswork, keeps you accountable on the days you would rather skip and spots the small technique faults that turn into injuries later.

Don's background in Jeet Kune Do and martial arts conditioning means sessions stay athletic and varied. You will lift, but you will also learn to move well, breathe properly and build the kind of fitness that carries into everyday life.

What a typical session looks like

  1. Warm-up and mobility (10 minutes): joint prep for the work ahead.
  2. Main strength block (25 minutes): your key lifts or calisthenics progressions.
  3. Conditioning finisher (10 minutes): intervals, carries or pad work.
  4. Cool-down and review (5 minutes): stretch, log the session, plan the next one.

Your first four weeks

The first month sets the tone for everything that follows, so it is planned carefully. Most new clients follow this pattern.

WeekFocusWhat you will notice
1Assessment, technique basics, building the habitSome muscle soreness, a clear plan and a routine that fits your week
2Learning the main movement patterns properlyExercises start to feel smoother and more controlled
3First real progressions: more weight, reps or harder variationsBetter energy and sleep, and your first strength gains
4Check-in and retestMeasurable progress you can see in your numbers

After week four, your programme is updated based on what the retest shows. That cycle of train, test and adjust is what keeps progress going long after the beginner gains slow down.

Training around your schedule

Training in Nairobi comes with its own challenges: unpredictable traffic, long working days and weekends full of family and church commitments. A good programme accepts that reality instead of pretending it does not exist.

  • Flexible timing: early-morning sessions beat the traffic, and evening slots suit people who cannot train before work.
  • Short-session backups: if a meeting runs late, you get a 20-minute version of the workout so the week is not lost.
  • Travel plans: heading upcountry or out of town? You get a bodyweight plan you can do in a hotel room or a compound.
  • Realistic nutrition: ugali, githeri, sukuma, chapati, nyama choma. You do not have to give up the food you love. You learn how much of it fits your goal.

Nutrition guidance

You cannot out-train a diet that works against you, but you also do not need expensive supplements or imported meal plans. Nutrition coaching covers five basics:

  1. Protein at every meal: eggs, beans, ndengu, fish, chicken, beef or milk.
  2. Plenty of vegetables: sukuma wiki, cabbage, spinach, tomatoes and whatever is in season at the market.
  3. Portion control for starches: ugali, rice and chapati are fine, in amounts that match your goal.
  4. Water first: most people are under-hydrated, especially in the dry season.
  5. Consistency over perfection: a good week beats a perfect day followed by giving up.

Want to go beyond the basics? Pair personal training with calisthenics coaching to build relative strength, or boxing fitness for high-energy conditioning.

Checklist: getting the most from personal training

  • Tell your coach about any injuries, conditions or medication before your first session.
  • Sleep seven to eight hours where possible. Recovery is where the progress happens.
  • Eat a light meal one to two hours before training.
  • Log your sessions so you can see your progress over months.
  • Do the short homework sessions between coached workouts.
  • Be honest at check-ins. Your plan can only adapt to what your coach knows.

Personal Training FAQs

How much does a personal trainer cost in Nairobi?

Prices depend on how many sessions you book per week and where you train. Message D Dynamic Fitness on WhatsApp for current session and package rates.

Do I need to be fit before I start personal training?

No. Programmes are built from your current level. Complete beginners are welcome, and the first session is an assessment so we know exactly where to start.

How many sessions a week do I need?

Two to three coached sessions a week is enough for most people to see steady progress. We can add a home or solo plan for the days in between.

Can personal training help with fat loss?

Yes. Fat loss comes from consistent training plus sensible eating. You get both a training programme and simple nutrition guidance built around food you already eat.

Is personal training suitable if I have an old injury?

Often, yes. Tell your coach about any injury at the assessment. Programmes avoid aggravating movements and build strength around the problem area. For recent or serious injuries, get medical clearance first.

Can I train with a friend or partner?

Yes. Partner sessions share the cost and keep you both accountable. Ask on WhatsApp about shared session rates.
