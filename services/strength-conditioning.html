Strength and conditioning is how athletes prepare their bodies for sport: more power, more speed, more endurance and fewer injuries. You do not need to be a professional athlete to benefit from training like one.

Don Michael Ondiro is a certified fitness and performance coach. At D Dynamic Fitness, performance coaching combines barbell and dumbbell strength work, calisthenics, plyometrics and conditioning into one plan.

What performance coaching covers

  • Maximal strength: squats, deadlifts, presses and rows with sound technique.
  • Power: jumps, throws and explosive bodyweight drills.
  • Speed and agility: acceleration, change of direction and footwork.
  • Conditioning: energy-system work matched to your sport.
  • Injury resilience: mobility, single-leg strength and joint prep.

How programmes are structured

Training runs in blocks of four to six weeks. Each block has a clear focus, such as building a base, increasing strength or peaking for a season, and ends with a quick retest so you can see the progress in numbers.

Strength coaching pairs well with mobility and flexibility training for recovery, and with one-on-one personal training if you prefer every session fully coached.

The building blocks of athletic performance

Every sport demands a different mix of qualities, but all of them sit on the same foundation. Performance coaching works on each layer in order:

  1. Movement quality: squat, hinge, lunge, push, pull and carry patterns done well.
  2. Strength: the ability to produce force, the base for everything else.
  3. Power: producing that force quickly, for jumping, sprinting, striking and throwing.
  4. Speed and agility: applying power in the right direction at the right time.
  5. Sport-specific conditioning: fitness that matches the demands of your game.

Skipping steps is the most common reason athletes get injured. A footballer chasing sprint speed without single-leg strength is a hamstring injury waiting to happen.

Testing and tracking

At the start and end of each block we test a few simple markers, chosen for your sport:

  • Strength: rep maximums on key lifts or strict pull-ups and push-ups.
  • Power: broad jump or vertical jump.
  • Speed: 10 m and 20 m sprint times.
  • Conditioning: a repeat-effort test matched to your sport.
  • Mobility: simple range-of-motion screens for hips, ankles and shoulders.

Recovery matters as much as training

Strength is built in training but gained during recovery. Programmes include guidance on sleep, hydration, eating enough protein and planning lighter weeks. If you play matches on the weekend, heavy sessions are scheduled early in the week so you arrive fresh.

Performance coaching is popular with runners, cyclists and weekend athletes in Karen and Kileleshwa. For extra striking power, combine it with Jeet Kune Do or boxing fitness.

Strength & Conditioning FAQs

Do you train teams as well as individuals?

Yes, by arrangement. Contact D Dynamic Fitness on WhatsApp to discuss team or small-group performance sessions.

Will strength training make me slower?

No. Well-planned strength training makes athletes faster and more powerful. Programmes balance heavy work with speed and mobility.

Can I do this during my competitive season?

Yes. In-season training is adjusted to maintain strength while keeping you fresh for matches and competitions.

At what age can young athletes start strength training?

Supervised, technique-focused strength training is suitable for teenagers. The emphasis is on movement quality and bodyweight skills before heavy loads.

How many sessions a week do athletes need?

Two to three strength and conditioning sessions a week is typical, depending on your sport's training and match schedule.
